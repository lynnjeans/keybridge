• Windows shortcuts: your Windows habits, as they are, on the Mac. For example, Ctrl+C/V/Z copy, paste and undo, Home/End jump to the start and end of the line, and Alt+Tab switches apps.
  • Each keyboard its own way: a MacBook's keyboard and a PC keyboard can each press Ctrl, Win and Alt with their own keys.
  • Mouse: the wheel can scroll the Windows way without touching the trackpad, the side buttons go back and forward, and fn or Ctrl+scroll zooms pages.
  • Clipboard history: ⌥V shows what you copied recently; with the Windows Key group on, so does Win+V on a PC keyboard.
  • Finder: Delete deletes, F2 renames, Ctrl+X/V move files, and the right-click menu creates a new document, copies a path or opens Terminal.
  • Windows: ⌥+arrows snaps the front window to half the screen or fills it, like Win+arrows.
  • Opening or saving a file: Ctrl+G jumps to the folder open in Finder, and Ctrl+Shift+G lists recent and favorite folders.
  • Ten languages, Open at Login, and automatic updates.
