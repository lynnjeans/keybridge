• Windows shortcuts: your Windows habits, as they are, on the Mac. For example, Ctrl+C/V/Z copy, paste and undo, Home/End jump to the start and end of the line, Ctrl+←/→ moves by word, and Alt+Tab switches apps.
  • Mouse: reverse the wheel direction, give the side buttons any function (back and forward, for example), and zoom pages with Ctrl+scroll.
  • Finder: Ctrl+X and Ctrl+V move files, among other Windows habits, and the right-click menu creates a new document, copies a path or opens Terminal.
  • Windows: ⌥+arrows snaps the front window to half the screen or fills it, like Win+arrows on Windows.
  • Clipboard history: ⇧⌘C shows what you copied recently, like Win+V. The shortcut can be changed, to ⌥V for example.
  • Opening or saving a file: Ctrl+G jumps to the folder open in Finder, and Ctrl+Shift+G lists recent and favorite folders.
